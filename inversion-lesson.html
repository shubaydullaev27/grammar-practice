Inversion

Flip the verb, raise the volume.

SpeakingWritingIELTS band 7+

How to make an inversion

Three patterns cover everything. Find the verb form in your sentence, then follow its colour.

1. Verb “be” (am, is, are, was, were)Swap the verb and the subject.
you are → are you
2. Already has an auxiliary or modalMove only the first auxiliary in front of the subject.
she has gone → has she gone
3. Simple tenses, any other verbAdd do / does / did and put it first.
I see → do I see
we arrived → did we arrive

Five times we use inversion

1. Negative adverbs at the start

Used for drama and emphasis. Formal. Tap any chip for its Uzbek and Russian meaning.
NeverSeldomRarelyHardly … whenScarcely … whenNo sooner … thanNot only … but alsoLittleNowhereIn no wayOn no accountUnder no circumstances
NORMALI have seldom seen such beautiful work.
INVERSIONSeldom have I seen such beautiful work.
NORMALWe arrived home and the police rang the bell.
INVERSIONNo sooner had we arrived home than the police rang the bell.
NORMALHe loves chocolate and he also smokes.
INVERSIONNot only does he love chocolate, but he also smokes.
NORMALHe didn’t know what was waiting.
INVERSIONLittle did he know!

2. “Only” + time or condition

The inversion comes in the second part of the sentence.
Only thenOnly laterOnly after …Only when …Only by …ingNot until …Not since …
NORMALI really believed he was safe when I saw John.
INVERSIONNot until I saw John did I really believe he was safe.
NORMALWe could afford to eat only by working hard.
INVERSIONOnly by working extremely hard could we afford to eat.

3. Conditionals without “if”

Very formal. Works with Had, Were, Should.
NORMALIf I had been there, this wouldn’t have happened.
INVERSIONHad I been there, this wouldn’t have happened.
NORMALIf I were you, I would apologise.
INVERSIONWere I you, I would apologise.
NORMALIf you should need help, call me.
INVERSIONShould you need help, call me.

4. Place first

Literary, great for storytelling and descriptions.
NORMALThe knights came round the corner.
INVERSIONRound the corner came the knights.
NORMALAll the money we had lost was on the table.
INVERSIONOn the table was all the money we had lost.

5. So + adjective … that

Shows an extreme quality. Descriptive.
NORMALThe food was so delicious that we ate every bite.
INVERSIONSo delicious was the food that we ate every bite.
NORMALThe girl was so beautiful that nobody could talk of anything else.
INVERSIONSo beautiful was the girl that nobody could talk of anything else.

Where to use it

In speaking

  • Telling a story: Little did I know…
  • Presentations and speeches
  • IELTS Speaking Part 2 and 3, for range and emphasis
  • Job interviews: Never have I missed a deadline.
Use 1–2 per answer. In casual chat it can sound too stiff.

In writing

  • Essays: Not only does this cause…, but it also…
  • Formal emails: Should you require…
  • Stories and descriptions
  • IELTS Writing Task 2, for grammatical range
Use 2–3 per piece. More than that sounds unnatural.
Watch out. No inversion when the adverb only modifies a noun: Hardly anyone passed the exam. Also keep the main verb in its base form after did: Never did I see, not saw.
