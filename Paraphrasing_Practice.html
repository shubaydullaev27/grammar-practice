Six paraphrasing patterns

In each task you get a sentence and a key word. Your new sentence must keep the same meaning and must use the key word (do not change it). Check these patterns first.

1

UNLESS = if … not

unless+affirmative verb
Unless you hurry, you’ll miss the bus. = If you don’t hurry, you’ll miss the bus.
You won’t pass unless you study. = You won’t pass if you don’t study.

Rule: unless already means “if … not”, so the verb after it is positive. Never write “unless you don’t”. “Without + noun” becomes “unless + clause”.

2

OBJECTION = didn’t mind / didn’t oppose / wasn’t against

subject+didn’t have / raise / make+any objection(s)+to + noun / V-ing
subject+had / raised / made+no objection(s)+to + noun / V-ing
The boss didn’t have any objection to us leaving early. = The boss didn’t mind us leaving early.
The boss had no objection to us leaving early. = same meaning, positive verb + no.
She raised no objections to the plan. = She didn’t oppose the plan.
The neighbours didn’t say they were against building the playground. = The neighbours didn’t have any objection to building the playground.

Use: to is a preposition, so a verb after it takes -ing (objection to leaving, not “to leave”). Other verbs: raise / make / voice / put forward an objection. Avoid: ✗ didn’t have no objection (double negative), ✗ objection against / with (we say objection to).

3

DEMAND = insist on

subject+demand+to + V1 / noun / that + clause
They demanded to see the manager. = They insisted on seeing the manager.
The staff demanded a pay rise. = The staff insisted on getting a pay rise.
He demanded that he be allowed to speak.

Careful: after demand we do not use -ing. We say “demand to see”, not “demand seeing”. After “insist” we say “insist on seeing”.

4

IN CASE = because it might happen

in case+present simple (future) / past simple (past)
I’ll take a map in case we get lost. = because we might get lost.
She packed a torch in case the power went out.

Rule: in case is followed by the present simple (not “will”) when we talk about the future, and by the past simple after a past main verb. “In case” = to be ready; “if” = only when it happens.

5

MUST HAVE + V3 = I’m sure it happened

subject+must have+V3
He must have forgotten about it. = I’m certain he forgot about it.
They must have left already. = The food is untouched, so I’m sure they left.

Use: logical deduction about the past. V3 is the past participle (forgotten, booked, left). For “It was Harry” we say must have been Harry.

6

IF / WHETHER = reported yes/no questions

asked+if / whether+subject+verb (one tense back)
“Did you lock the door?” → She asked if I had locked the door.
“Do you know Dr Smith?” → He asked whether she knew Dr Smith.

Rule: use normal word order (no do/did, no question mark), move the tense one step back (did → had + V3, do → did, have done → had done) and change pronouns: you → I / he / she.
