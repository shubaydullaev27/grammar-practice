1. What it was and why I bought it

Less preferred answer

I bought roller skates a long time ago. I wanted to do exercise because I was lazy and not healthy.

Preferred answer

, I bought a pair of roller skates, mainly because I wanted to . I'd been pretty inactive for months and felt I had to do something about it.

What changed: a spoken time connector, an idiom, and the past perfect continuous ("I'd been") to give background.
2. Why I wanted them

Less preferred answer

I saw many people skating in the park and I like it. I wanted to be like them.

Preferred answer

What changed: an extra detail, a precise verb instead of "like", and a personal feeling that makes the story sound real.

Part 3: discussion questions

Give a developed answer, not just a reason

For "Why do people…?" questions, use this order:

OpinionSpeculate: you can't know for certain, so sound thoughtful.
ReasonExplain why your opinion makes sense.
ExampleAdd a personal or real-life example.
ConclusionFinish with a short summary.

Useful ways to speculate:

I guess it could be because…I suppose it may well be that…I reckon they might…It's not uncommon for people to…

Why do some people prefer not to shop online?

Less preferred answer

Because they don't like online shopping. It's not safe and they want to see the things.

Preferred answer

OpinionI suppose it may well be that they don't feel safe handing over their card details.
ReasonIt's not uncommon for people to hear about online fraud, so the worry is understandable.
ExampleMy uncle, for instance, only pays cash now after a friend's account was emptied.
ConclusionSo I'd say trust is the main thing holding them back.
The less preferred answer gives one idea with no support. The preferred answer speculates, explains, gives an example and closes.
