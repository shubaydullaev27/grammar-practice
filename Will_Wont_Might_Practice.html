Example
PositivePeople will use public transport more.
Public transport might be more crowded soon.
NegativeDriving won’t be dangerous one day.
Workers might not travel to offices in the future.
QuestionWill planes fly without pilots soon?
With might we don’t use a direct question: Do you think trains might become cheaper?
Remember
  • The form never changes: I will, he will, they will (no -s).
  • After these words we use the verb without to: She will go.  She will to go.
  • will → ’ll after pronouns (I’ll, he’ll), but not after nouns: The team will win.

4. How to choose: 3 quick steps

  1. Is the speaker sure? Look for clue words: I’m sure, definitely, always, never, I promise → sure. Not sure, yet, depends, can’t promise → not sure.
  2. If sure: is the result positive or negative? Good reason → will. Negative reason (closed, broken, too busy, hates) → won’t.
  3. If not sure: which possibility does the sentence talk about? Something that can happen → might. A problem or a reason against it → might not.
Sure or not sure? Kim hates fish. I’m sure she won’t eat it. (sure, negative reason)
Anna might go. She hasn’t decided. (not sure)
might or might not? The weather looks bad. It might rain. (it can happen)
The train has a problem. It might not arrive on time. (it can fail)

5. Extra tips

  • probably comes after will and before won’t → a little less sure: He will probably pass.  She probably won’t come.
  • definitely makes it more sure: It will definitely change.  It definitely won’t change.
  • I think… / I don’t think… introduce a prediction with will: I think he’ll come.  I don’t think they’ll win.
