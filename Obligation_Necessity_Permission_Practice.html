Remember
  • must and can never change: he must, she can (no -s).
  • After must / can / have to use the base verb: She can go.  She can goes.
  • In questions we say Do you have to…?  (not Must you…?)

4. Careful! mustn’t ≠ don’t have to

mustn’t

= forbidden
You mustn’t park here.
→ Don’t do it. It is not allowed.

don’t have to

= not necessary
You don’t have to pay.
→ It is free. You can pay, but you don’t need to.

5. Must or have to?

MUST

My own feeling
  • What I think or want:
    I must thank the teacher. (I really want to.)
    He must take a holiday. (I think he needs it.)
  • Instructions and notices, especially in writing:
    Students must arrive before 9 a.m.

HAVE TO

A rule from other people
  • A rule or an order:
    We have to do homework every night. (The teachers give us homework.)
    They have to play sport. (It’s the rule at their school.)
  • Questions:
    Do you have to leave?

6. Can and can’t

  • can = it is OK, it is allowed.  can’t (or mustn’t) = it is not allowed.
  • Asking: Can we work outside today?  Answering: Yes, you can. / No, you can’t.
  • Rules: They can wear their own clothes after school.  We can’t take more than an hour for lunch.
  • Warning: You mustn’t forget to write your essay. (= Don’t forget!)

7. Question words: Do / Does / Can

When the gap is at the start of a question, look at the words after it.

… you have to …? Use Do / Does:
I / you / we / they → Do
he / she / my sister → Does
Does he have to wear a tie?
… we sit here, please? No have to: permission → Can
Can I use your phone, please?
Short answers: Yes, you can. / No, you can’t.  Yes, you do. / No, you don’t.

8. Quick check

  1. Is it necessary? → must / have to
  2. Is it not necessary? → don’t have to
  3. Is it forbidden? → mustn’t / can’t
  4. Is it OK? → can
