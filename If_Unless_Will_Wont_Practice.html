Grammar Summary

First Conditional: if / unless + will / won't

We use if and unless to introduce a possible condition, and will / won't to show the likely future result.

Structure
if / unless + Present Simple , will / won't + base verb
Important: after if / unless, normally use the Present Simple — not will.
If you study, you will pass. → a possible condition, a likely result
Unless you study, you won't pass. → = If you don't study, you won't pass.
❌ If it will rain, we will stay home. → no will after if
✅ If it rains, we will stay home. → present simple after if

The four words

if

Introduces a possible condition.

If you study, you will pass.

unless

Means "if' not" — a negative condition.

Unless you study, you won't pass.

will

Shows the likely, positive future result.

If it rains, we will stay home.

won't

Will not — usually a negative future result.

If it rains, we won't go out.
