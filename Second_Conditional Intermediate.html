Second Conditional

We use the Second Conditional to talk about imaginary, unlikely, or unreal situations in the present or future.

Form: If + Past Simple, would + base verb
If I had more money, I would travel more.
She would buy it if it were cheaper.

Negative:

If I didn't have work, I would go with you.
He wouldn't be late if he left earlier.
Note: With I / he / she / it, we often use were instead of was in formal English: If I were you, I would apologize.
