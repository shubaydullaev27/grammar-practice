Grammar Summary

Third Conditional

We use the Third Conditional to talk about an imaginary past: an unreal condition and its unreal result, both already over.

Structure
If had + past participle , would have + past participle
The condition never happened, so the result never happened either.
If you had studied, you would have passed. → you didn't study, so you didn't pass
If she hadn't been late, she wouldn't have missed the bus. → she was late, so she missed it
❌ If you would have studied, you would have passed. → no would in the if-clause

Also possible in the result clause

could have + past participle → ability/possibility: If he had trained harder, he could have won.
might have + past participle → possibility: If they had left earlier, they might have caught the train.
