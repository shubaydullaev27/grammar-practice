1. The main idea

Past Simple

A finished action
I made a phone call.
Think: ✔ done

Past Continuous

An action in progress at a past moment
I was sitting on the train.
Think: ⟳ not finished yet

3. When do we use them?

Past Simple

  • Finished actions:
    He flew to Egypt three times.
  • Actions one after another:
    I got into the car, shut the door and started the engine.
  • Completed actions with a time:
    She waited for him for 30 minutes.

Past Continuous

  • An action in progress at a particular time:
    At 3 p.m., she was still packing her bags.
  • The longer, background action:
    While I was sitting on the train, …

4. Using them together

A long action (past continuous) is interrupted by a short action (past simple).

While I was sitting on the train …
… I made a phone call.

Clue words
suddenlywhen (+ short action)yesterdaylast night / weekend / Fridayas soon asbecauseand … (one after another)
We arrived at the station at 6 p.m. yesterday.
Tip
  • suddenly shows the short action that interrupts: I was dreaming when the alarm suddenly rang.
  • when can start the short action or be followed by it: When you called me, I was playing a game.
  • as soon as = one action, then the next → two past simple verbs: As soon as the film ended, everybody clapped.

6. Careful! Stative verbs

Stative verbs are NOT used in the past continuous. They describe states, not actions. Common stative verbs: be, know, like, love, want, need, understand, believe, prefer
✔ He did a puzzle while we were on holiday.
✘ He did a puzzle while we were being on holiday.

7. Quick check

  1. Is it a finished action? → past simple
  2. Was it in progress at that time? → past continuous
  3. Is the verb a state (know, like, be)? → past simple only
