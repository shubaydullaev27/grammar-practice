Explanation

Causative have / get

We use the causative to say that someone else does a job for us (a service we arrange), or that something happens to us without us doing it. The verbs are have and get, and the pattern after them decides the meaning.

1

Something is done for you

Subject+have / get+object+V3
I had my shoes repaired. = Someone repaired them for me.
We will get the room painted. = Someone will paint it for us.

Use: services or jobs you arrange. V3 is the past participle (repaired, painted, stolen, broken). Get is often more informal.

2

Say who does the job

Subject+have+person+V1
Subject+get+person+to + V1
I had a mechanic check the engine.
I got a mechanic to check the engine.

V1 is the base verb. After have + person do not add to. After get + person use to. Get can suggest that you persuaded someone or managed to arrange the action.

3

Something unexpected happens

Subject+have+object+V3
She had her purse stolen. = Someone stole it from her.
They had their gate damaged. = Their gate was damaged.

The same form can describe an unwanted experience. It does not mean the person arranged it.

4

Change have / get, not the pattern

TensePatternExample
Pasthad / got + object + V3I had my car washed.
Presenthave / has / get / gets + object + V3She has her hair cut every month.
After will / should / musthave / get + object + V3We should have the roof inspected.
Present perfecthave / has had + object + V3He has had the lock replaced.

Careful: in He has had the lock replaced, the first has marks the tense; had is the causative verb.

5

A quick decision

Object first?have the wall painted
Use V3.
Person first?have a painter paint
get a painter to paint
Use V1 after have, to + V1 after get.

Look immediately after have / get to decide.
